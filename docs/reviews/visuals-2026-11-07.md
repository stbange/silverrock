# Visual review of the city (2026-11-07)

A review of how the city looks after M5 and the playability pass, judged from the gameplay
screenshots in `docs/screenshots/m5/` and from the renderer (`src/render/sr_scene.c`,
`src/render/sr_mesh.c`, `src/render/gl/sr_gl.c`). It compares the result with the art direction in
`docs/concept/01-game-design.md` §6.5, §6.6 and §16. The plan that answers it is the OpenSpec change
`openspec/changes/improve-city-atmosphere`.

## Summary

The city works as a space: it is lit, it rains, people walk and districts have their own colours.
But it does not yet feel like a place. Every view is two walls of identical window grids, an empty
road and a dark slot at the end. The art direction promises shopfronts, street furniture by district,
billboards that react to the corporations, steam, wet reflections and the Pall overhead. None of
these exist yet. Most of the detail is far above the player's head, and the street, where the
player spends nearly all their time, has the least.

## What works

- **District colours.** The fog and neon palettes tell districts apart (magenta strip, teal
  residential, cold Spire).
- **Crowds and traffic.** Walk cycles, umbrellas and cars give the street life at a distance.
- **The retro pipeline.** Low resolution, dithering, bloom, fog that neon shines through, and the
  rotating holo-logos over corporate headquarters all hold up.
- **Determinism.** Everything is generated from the seed with no art assets, so the download stays
  small. The plan keeps it that way.

## Findings

### V1 Facades read as noise
- Every building uses one of three window textures, tiled the same way on all four sides.
- Each texture always starts at the same corner, so neighbouring buildings repeat the exact same
  pattern of lit windows.
- 38 % of windows are lit everywhere, at every hour, in every kind of building.
- The fragment shader adds a highlight boost to bright texels, so every lit window clips to white.
  In the Spire (`04_spire_towers.png`) and at sunrise (`17_sunrise_ending.png`) the towers look like
  TV static.

### V2 Every building is a box
- Buildings are one box each, with flat roofs and no setbacks, ledges, cornices or crowns. Only
  corporate headquarters get stacked tiers.
- Lots are square and inset by a random 2–6 m, so street walls have no rhythm.
- Rooftop props (one box and one antenna) appear on a third of the tall buildings. Nobody sees
  them from the street.

### V3 The ground floor is the same as the fortieth
- There are no shopfronts, doors, lobbies, shutters or stoops.
- Places of interest get a sign and an awning. Every other building meets the pavement with
  the same window grid as its top floor.

### V4 The streets are empty
- A street is an asphalt strip with a centre line, plus a pavement slab that floats 12 cm up with
  no kerb face.
- Each block has one lamp, at its corner.
- There are no crosswalks, traffic lights, manholes, drains, bins, benches, vending machines,
  hydrants, bollards, parked cars, stalls or overhead cables.
- Barrel fires in the Barrens and Undercity are the only street props in the game.

### V5 Almost no signage
- Signs exist only on places of interest: an icon over the door.
- Shop names are not drawn. The spec "Readable signage" asks for the generated name in a bitmap
  font.
- No billboards, blade signs, holo-ads or tickers. §6.5 promises megacorp ads that get vandalised
  after a scandal and multiply after a win.

### V6 The sky and the horizon are empty
- The sky is the flat fog colour. The Pall, "a glow over the brightest districts" (§6.6), is not
  drawn.
- No searchlights, blimps or drones fill the space above the street.
- Each street ends in a dark slot: the skyline ring sits far away, and nothing stands between the
  last streamed chunk and that ring.

### V7 Rain does not make the street wet
- When it rains, the lights get brighter on the ground, and that is all.
- There are no reflections, puddles, splashes, drips from awnings or steam.
- Rain streaks are thin and sparse, so even "downpour" (`05_rain.png`) is hard to see.

### V8 Districts only differ in colour
- The geometry is the same in every district. Only the facade texture changes: brick in the Old
  Quarter, metal in Industrial and the Docks, glass in the Spire and Campus.
- No district has its own architecture: no lantern strings or tiled roofs in the Old Quarter, no
  cranes or containers at the Docks, no stacks or tanks in Industrial, no shanties in the Undercity,
  no skybridges in the Spire.

### V9 The vertical layers are missing
- Elevated highways are a flat slab 11 m up, with no pillars, barriers, lights or traffic.
- The Undercity is not under anything.
- There are no skyways between towers, and the elevated metro is not shown (§6.1 step 3).

### V10 Little moves
- What moves today: people, cars, aerodynes, rain and the rotating logos.
- Nothing flickers, scrolls, steams, smokes or blinks in rhythm, so a still screenshot and a running
  game look the same.

### V11 Lights don't pool
- Lighting is per vertex, and a building face is two triangles up to 300 m tall. The ground is one
  quad per street strip.
- A street lamp's light cannot show as a pool on the pavement or a splash on the wall. It only
  shifts the colour of the whole face a little.

### V12 Contrast is upside down
- Ambient light is high, so walls and roads sit in a mid-grey haze, while every lit window is pure
  white.
- Neon, which should be the brightest and most saturated thing on screen, competes with thousands
  of windows and loses.

### V13 The texture array is full, and one sign is wrong
- The texture array holds 32 layers of 128 px. The base materials take 12, and the 21 sign icons
  start at layer 12.
- So the icon for SR_POI_HOME would sit at layer 32, past the end of the array. GL clamps it to
  layer 31, and the home building shows the media icon.
- No layer is free for new materials or sprites.

## The vision (added after the review)

The city should always be dark:
- **Long nights, short days.** Nights are long and days are short. Dawns and dusks show a red
  glow under the clouds.
- **Dark, overcast days.** Days are overcast under dark clouds, so neon is always the main light.
- **Rain and fog.** It rains nearly all the time and there is always fog. The player cannot see
  far, but distant neon glows through the fog.
- **Gloomy.** The mood is gloomy.

The plan was reshaped around this. Two new phases come right after the foundations:
- **The gloom:** the light cycle, the climate and neon through fog.
- **Wet streets.**

## What the plan does

The change `improve-city-atmosphere` has eleven phases, ordered so that each one makes the city
look better on its own:

1. Foundations: texture atlas, sprites, contrast.
2. The gloom: light cycle, climate, neon through fog, cloud deck.
3. Wet streets.
4. Facades.
5. Silhouettes.
6. The street.
7. Signs and adverts.
8. Sky details.
9. District kits and the vertical layers.
10. Motion.
11. Lighting and post effects, then verification with a fixed set of reference views.

It stays procedural (no downloaded art), deterministic from the seed, and inside the frame budgets
on phones. The detail is derived in the render layer from the existing city data, so seeds, saves
and the city digest do not change.
