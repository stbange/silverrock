# Playtest notes: 16 long runs after M5 (2026-11-07)

Sixteen characters were each played for 26 in-game weeks (half a year), day by day. Every run
logged a daily timeline and the story events. These notes judge the game design as it stands after
M5: what works, what breaks the fantasy, and what to change. Numbers come from the runs. Opinions
are marked as suggestions.

## How the runs were played

```bash
./build/rel/sr_headless --playtest 16 out/   # 16 runs x 26 weeks; out/runNN_<bot>.csv (daily) and .log (events)
```

The runs use two kinds of bot:

- **Reachable-play bots (8 runs).** They only do what a human can do in the current build: walk to
  the home district's fixer, take the job on offer, do it with one approach (ghost, face, hammer or
  netrunner, or street gigs only), report back, eat, sleep and pay rent. They take the story
  choices on the terminal: the retainer, defection when the patron turns hostile, and an ending.
- **Story bots (8 runs).** Like the above, but they also take the patron's contract jobs and black
  ops through the script API. They show the campaign as designed.

**Limits.** Bots resolve missions through the simulated mission path, not the 3D levels. They never
shop, trade shares or dive into the Matrix. The runs measure the macro game (pacing, economy,
progression, the story and how visible the faction game is), not moment-to-moment feel.

## What works

- **Completion and stability.** Every run that went beyond street gigs reached an ending, between
  days 85 and 104. There were no soft-locks, crashes or evictions.
- **A busy world.** The world feels busy without the player. There were 450–670 news items per
  run and about 30 story arcs per run. Factions take over facilities, gangs replace each other,
  and elections and curfews happen on their own.
- **Endings vary.** The runs ended in Loyal (3), Kingmaker (8), Burn the Board (3) and Defector (1).
  The ending depended on how the player behaved towards the patron.
- **Loud play has visible consequences.** Hammer runs made the news as "shadowrunner blamed" 18–26
  times; quiet runs did 1–4 times.

## Findings, by impact

### 1. The corporate game is out of reach in real play (critical)

At a fixer, pressing E accepts the first job the fixer generates. There is no list to choose from
and no way to see the target, client or pay first. Jobs from the corporate game are posted to the
board, but no screen offers them:

- contract steps through the fixer, board, gang or handler channels;
- the handler's patron jobs;
- black ops (T4);
- the public job board.

The player hears about handler jobs ("Check your jobs") but cannot take them. Only the scripted
story bots ever did.

*Suggestion:*
- Add a job list at fixers and a **Jobs** tab on the terminal.
- Show all offers with client, target, pay, tier, deadline and the channel they came from.
- Route handler and gang contracts to the terminal, and accept with E.
- This single change is what makes M5 playable.

### 2. Fixer jobs are not part of the faction game

The design goal is "every job the player sees is a step in some faction's operation". Even the
story bots did only 11–17 contract jobs out of about 380 jobs each. Everything else was a random
template from the fixer with no effect on the board.

*Suggestion:* the fixer's daily offers should come from open contracts in that district first,
topped up with generic street gigs only when there are not enough.

### 3. Progression is far too fast

| Tier | Typical day reached |
|---|---|
| Runner | 8–10 |
| Operator | about 15–20 |
| Knight | 31–55 (31–36 for the strong builds) |
| Legend | 45–75 |

The "rise from nobody" is over within the first month of a six-month save. The cause is volume:
about 2 jobs a day, each at the edge of the player's tier, with Street Cred added for every one.

*Suggestions:*
- Make each tier need many more points (diminishing returns per tier).
- Add Street Cred only for notable jobs: a clean ghost run, a high-profile target, the news.
- Make Street Cred decay when the player is idle.
- Tie Knight and Legend to story beats rather than volume.

### 4. Money pressure is gone by week two

Rent is 300 a week. By week 2 the strong builds held 20–80 times that in cash, and by week 26 they
held 0.4–1.9 million. Black ops pay 10,000–50,000 each, and nothing in the game costs that much.
Rent was never missed in any run, so the survival layer from M2 stops mattering almost at once.

*Suggestions:*
- **Money sinks** that fit the setting:
  - better housing that costs real money;
  - gear and cyberware upkeep;
  - medical bills after missions;
  - fixer cuts, bribes for Heat clean-up, fees for intel and safehouses;
  - stakes in the market.
- Make black ops rare (one a week) and pay them in favours, intel or Board reveals as well as cash.
- Scale rent with the housing tier, and let the landlord raise it as the player's notoriety grows.

### 5. There is no real danger

- **Simulated missions can't kill.** They floor health at 1 HP (`sr_job_execute`), so only 3D
  missions can kill. Every risky run hit 1 HP and walked on: zero flatlines in 16 runs.
- **Heat goes nowhere.** It peaked at 2–5 with no lasting effect.
- **Hunts do nothing.** A hostile patron "hunting the player" starts hunt operations that no game
  code handles (`SR_OP_HUNT`): nobody ever comes.

*Suggestions:*
- Let simulated missions carry real risk: injury, medical downtime and bills, and death at
  hardcore difficulty.
- Turn hunts into events: an ambush on the street, a hit squad at home, or a bounty that puts
  gangers on the player's trail.
- Make high Heat bring checkpoints and raids.

### 6. Patron loyalty means nothing (defection churn)

Reachable-play bots switched patrons 7–13 times in 26 weeks, sometimes back to a corp that was still
hunting them. The causes:

- **Betrayal by accident.** Ordinary fixer jobs sometimes target the patron, and the player is not
  told. Two noisy jobs (−25 trust each) turn the patron hostile.
- **Instant reset.** Defecting resets trust to 40 straight away.
- **No memory.** A corp the player betrayed makes new offers a few days later.

*Suggestions:*
- Mark jobs against the patron, with a handler warning before accepting.
- Only lose trust when the patron learns of the job (evidence plus time), not instantly.
- A corp the player betrayed never takes them back.
- Allow one defection per campaign, or make the second one very expensive.
- Start trust low after a defection.

### 7. Act pacing is lopsided

| Act | Length in the runs |
|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–2 days |
| Act I | 1–3 weeks |
| Act II | always hits the 12-week limit (dominance never triggers; see the soak review) |
| Act III | 2–6 days, then the ending |
| After the ending | 78–97 days of play with no new goal |

The finale is shorter than a week of rent. After it, nothing changes except the news.

*Suggestions:*
- Act III should last at least two weeks: a climax of 3–5 beats that the player must take part in,
  plus a staged final job.
- Make dominance reachable (for example, takeovers raise a corp's long-term capital).
- Stage act transitions: a meeting with the handler, a news montage, a change in the city.
- After the ending, give the player new goals: a new patron cycle, the changed city's own
  problems, or a "New Game+" with the same character.

### 8. The story doesn't reach the player

- **The inbox is mostly reminders.** About 90 % of messages are the same three lines: two landlord
  rent reminders a week, and "Something deniable for you. Black-op rates." The inbox holds only the
  last 31 messages, so story messages scroll away.
- **The arcs are invisible.** Each run had about 30 story arcs, and none of them told the player
  anything: no messages, no named people or places.

*Suggestions:*
- **Fewer arcs, each one announced.** The first beat comes as a message from a cast member (the
  handler, the journalist, the rival runner) that names the people and the place.
- **Unique dialogue templates for each arc and beat.**
- **Fewer repeats in the inbox.** The landlord writes once a week, and repeated handler lines are
  merged.
- **A story log on the terminal** that keeps arcs and their outcomes.

### 9. News headlines need writing

Headlines made from the player's jobs read like debug text: "the Sabotage at Aurelian Grid Site 4",
and "...shadowrunner bla" is cut off at 64 characters. There are about three items a day,
which is about right for the billboard ticker.

*Suggestions:*
- Headline templates for each event type, plus a voice for each outlet (a corporate channel versus
  the pirate feed).
- Write headlines to fit the length limit.
- Have the press react to the player's growing notoriety.

### 10. Approaches are unbalanced

| Approach | Knight by day | Cash at week 26 |
|---|---|---|
| Netrunner | 55–88 | 450k–477k |
| Face | 34–50 | 594k–667k |
| Ghost, hammer, story | 33–44 | 419k–1.9M |

Hammer runs failed 3–6 times as often (45 and 91 failures) but paid nothing for it.

*Suggestions:*
- Reward clean work with bonuses, trust, less Heat and Board intel.
- Make loud work cost something lasting (see 5).
- Give netrunner runs more valuable paydata.

### 11. Street gigs stall the campaign

Players who only take street gigs (T0) accept the retainer on day 2 and then stay in Act I forever,
stuck at Pawn. That is fine as a choice, but nothing tells them.

*Suggestion:* the handler nudges them towards bigger work after two weeks.

### 12. Endings: hidden requirements and no real choice

- **Sunrise is never offered.** It needs the Pall Protocol scenario and Matrix depth 20, and the
  game never says so.
- **Kingmaker always crowns the same corp.** It is offered every time, but it automatically crowns
  the scenario's second corp; the player picks no one.
- **No ending sequence.** Each ending is one line of news.

*Suggestions:*
- Let the player choose whom to crown.
- Hint at Sunrise through the Ghost cast member.
- Give each ending a short sequence: three messages, a news montage, and the visible change in the
  city.

## Suggested order

1. **Quick wins:**
   - fewer repeated messages;
   - headline templates;
   - warn before jobs that hit the patron;
   - no re-hiring by corps the player betrayed;
   - real risk in simulated missions.
2. **The M5 playability pass:**
   - job selection UI with a terminal Jobs tab (finding 1);
   - fixer offers from contracts (finding 2);
   - hunts as events (finding 5).
3. **The balance pass:**
   - Street Cred curve (finding 3);
   - money sinks and black-op rarity (finding 4);
   - rewards by approach (finding 10).
4. **Story pacing:**
   - Act III length and staging;
   - arcs announced through the cast;
   - ending sequences;
   - post-ending goals (findings 7, 8 and 12).
